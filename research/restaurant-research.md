## Important local corrections

Benin is a member of the West African monetary union using the CFA franc. Use XOF as the currency code and FCFA/CFA as the customer-facing label, with prices in whole francs. This is supported by the supplied menu and the [BCEAO description of WAMU](https://www.bceao.int/en/content/presentation-wamu).

ARCEP announced the move from eight to ten national telephone digits on 30 November 2024, adding 01 before the previous number. This is national telephone-format context, not proof of the restaurant's WhatsApp registration. The user's subsequently confirmed WhatsApp destination is +229 50924184 and takes precedence over an inferred replacement. Keep telephone and WhatsApp settings separate if necessary and test the intended link. [ARCEP notice](https://arcep.bj/wp-content/uploads/2024/11/INFO-COM-A-TRADUIRE-ET-DIFFUSER.pdf)

Recommended language structure: French and English, with French as the provisional default. Local competitor sites reviewed use French, while NVO's supplied marketing is predominantly English. The preferred default remains an owner decision. Keep product names recognizable and translate descriptions, navigation, errors, coupon terms, and WhatsApp templates. Use Africa/Porto-Novo as the business timezone; store timestamps in UTC.

## Discovery and growth strategy

Start with accurate identity: owner-confirmed business name, address, Maps pin, hours, phone, menu and prices. Align the website and Google Business Profile. Link approved social accounts. Generate a sitemap, canonical URLs, share previews, and factual Restaurant structured data; validate it before launch. Google's documentation supports Restaurant markup for business information, but does not guarantee ranking or rich-result display. [Google LocalBusiness documentation](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/structured-data/local-business)

Create useful pages around the real menu and real service area. Research themes, not measured high-volume keywords: restaurant Akpakpa, restaurant Agblangandan, fruits de mer Cotonou, cuisine nigériane Cotonou, Nigerian food Cotonou, banga soup Cotonou, and meal delivery where actually offered. Avoid thin location pages and unverified “best restaurant” claims.

Use QR codes on packaging and tables, a direct menu link in social bios, and tagged campaign links. Build evergreen menu discovery first, then weekly content and controlled offers. Invite all customers to provide honest reviews without making rewards depend on positive reviews.

Proposed first experiments: a small meal-plus-drink offer, a referral reward released after a completed first order, and a limited lunch-period coupon drop. Keep campaign cost within an owner-set budget. Evaluate confirmed new customers and subsequent repeat orders, not page refreshes or coupon downloads alone. Discounts should complement food/service quality; constant giveaways can attract visitors who never become profitable regulars.
