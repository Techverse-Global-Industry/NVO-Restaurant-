# NVO Restaurant: 10 coupon strategies for customer growth

An operating guide for the owner and restaurant team. These are proposed experiments, not live campaigns or promises of results. All example amounts are in FCFA; replace them with NVO’s actual prices and food costs before publishing.

The goal is profitable first visits followed by return visits. Specials create appetite; coupons provide a clear reason to act. Keep the special’s food story on **Current specials** and its optional discount on **Coupons & rewards**.

## Know the controls before launching

| Admin control | What it actually does |
| --- | --- |
| Start / end dates | Set when new claims are available. An already issued card keeps its own activation and expiry dates. Ending a campaign does not shorten cards already issued. |
| Activation delay | Days between claiming and being allowed to redeem. At 0 it can be used immediately; at 2 the guest waits 48 hours. This is measured from the claim, not a previous purchase. |
| Validity after activation | How long the card remains usable once activated. A 2-day delay plus 7-day validity gives a 9-day claim-to-expiry interval. |
| Minimum order | The minimum eligible food subtotal needed for redemption. Delivery is agreed separately. |
| Maximum discount | Caps the saving per card. Set a cap for percentage campaigns. Zero means uncapped. |
| Total claims allowed | Total cards that may be issued, not a daily limit or the number of uses per guest. Expired cards still used one of those claim slots. |
| One claim per IP | NVO’s chosen strict rule: the same network cannot claim the same campaign twice, even from different browsers. Shared home, office and restaurant Wi-Fi can block different people. An IP is not proof of a person; changing networks can evade it. |
| Named card + unique code | Identifies an issued card for staff checking. Record the paid sale in the coupon desk; one card cannot be redeemed twice. |

Increasing the total claim limit opens more slots for **new eligible claimants**. It does not let a previous claimant or the same network claim that campaign again. To run a genuinely new offer later, create a new campaign with its own clear terms.

The system enforces the numeric discount, eligible free item, spend minimum, claim availability, activation, expiry and one-use rules. It does **not** automatically verify first-time customers, birthdays, weekday-only redemption, office membership, lapsed customers or a purchase prerequisite. Targeting language is not access control: arrange staff verification before promising a restricted offer. Published coupons are public; they are not private invitations.

## 1. A modest welcome offer

**Business purpose:** make trying NVO feel easy without teaching everyone to wait for a large discount.

**Example setup:** 10% off; minimum order 5,000; maximum saving 700; 30 total cards; activation 0 days; validity 7 days. Advertise to nearby residents using a tempting, real meal photo and a clear link to the coupon page.

**Staff action:** ask whether it is the guest’s first NVO visit when recording the paid sale. If you cannot consistently verify a first-visit restriction, describe it as a public “Discover NVO” offer that existing guests may also use.

**Measure:** new paying guests, discount cost per new paying guest and how many return within 30 days. Coupon claims alone are not acquired customers.

## 2. Give today’s guest a reason to return

**Business purpose:** turn a good first meal into a second visit.

**Example setup:** 500 off a future food order of at least 5,000; 30 cards; activation delay 2 days; validity 10 days. Staff introduce the next-visit card after a paid meal, explain its exact activation date and help the guest save it.

**Staff action:** confirm the previous paid purchase if that is part of the advertised terms. The delay itself does not prove a purchase happened. Claim on the guest’s own mobile connection where practical; strict shared-Wi-Fi limits still apply.

**Measure:** second paid visits and days between purchases. Compare with a similar period before the offer, allowing for seasonality.

## 3. Pair a kitchen special with a small extra

**Business purpose:** sell the special because it looks delicious, with a coupon as the final incentive.

**Example setup:** use a free-item coupon for one clearly identified, affordable menu accompaniment; minimum food spend 6,000; 20 cards; activation 0; validity 3 days. Photograph the special, publish it on its own page, then link to the optional coupon in the description or promotion.

**Staff action:** select the exact free menu item in the coupon editor and ensure it is included in the order. Current free-item logic removes the value of one eligible item; it does not automatically add the item to the basket. Verify costs and stock before publishing. A “special purchase required” restriction needs staff checks; it is not automatically enforced by the coupon engine.

**Measure:** special sales, accompaniment uptake and contribution after the extra’s actual cost. End new claims when the campaign is over, and honour already issued valid cards.

## 4. Turn quiet periods into planned visits

**Business purpose:** bring orders into genuinely quiet periods rather than discounting already busy service.

**Example setup:** trial 10% off with a 600 cap, 5,000 minimum, 20 claims, activation 0 and validity 2 days. Open claims shortly before a quieter period and announce the visit terms clearly.

**Staff action:** weekday or lunch-hour restrictions are not automatic. Use a general short-validity offer unless the team can verify the service window consistently. An expiry of “2 days after claiming” is not the same as “Tuesday lunch only”.

**Measure:** paid orders and contribution in the targeted service period, compared with similar days. Check whether guests simply moved an order from a busier full-price period.

## 5. Make a shared office order worthwhile

**Business purpose:** reach nearby workplaces and increase the value of one order.

**Example setup:** a fixed 1,500 saving on a food subtotal of at least 15,000; 15 cards; activation 0; validity 5 days. Ask one office organiser to assemble the group order and use one card.

**Staff action:** confirm kitchen capacity and collection or delivery arrangements. The minimum spend is enforceable; office affiliation is not. One organiser fits the strict IP rule better than asking every colleague on the same Wi-Fi to claim separately. Do not promise a discount for every person in a group.

**Measure:** average paid basket, number of meals per order and how many workplaces reorder without a discount.

## 6. Encourage an extra meal, not an oversized discount

**Business purpose:** raise basket value through a spend threshold slightly above the usual order size.

**Example setup:** if measured usual spend is about 4,500, test 600 off from 6,000; 25 claims; activation 0; validity 7 days. The 4,500 figure is an example, not NVO’s measured average.

**Staff action:** suggest relevant menu additions without pressuring the guest. Keep prices and the minimum visible in the terms. Do not raise base prices to manufacture a saving.

**Measure:** increase in average paid basket and contribution per order. A larger basket is useful only if the extra contribution exceeds the discount and extra service cost.

## 7. Let happy guests introduce NVO

**Business purpose:** make recommendations easy to act on.

**Example setup:** choose a public referral campaign, such as 10% off from 5,000 with a 700 cap, activation 0 and validity 7 days. In Restaurant settings, enable referrals, select that campaign and set a realistic claim window, for example 60 minutes after opening the invitation.

**Staff action:** explain that the invitation claim window and the card’s redemption validity are different clocks. Track referred paid visits from the saved referral association. The present system does not automatically pay or issue a reward to the person who shared the link, and the public campaign is not referral-exclusive. Any advocate reward requires a separate, documented staff process after a verified paid visit.

**Measure:** invited guests who actually pay, cost per referred paid guest and subsequent repeat visits. Link opens are not sales.

## 8. Bring back subscribed guests who have gone quiet

**Business purpose:** reconnect with people who enjoyed NVO but have not returned recently.

**Example setup:** a public “Welcome back to the table” campaign, 500 off from 5,000; 20 claims; activation 0; validity 5 days. Send a matching special and coupon link to opted-in WhatsApp customers.

**Staff action:** the current publishing flow can message subscribed customers; automatic selection of customers inactive for 30 days is not implemented. A lapsed-only audience requires an additional segmentation feature or an authorised manual process. Do not describe a public offer as exclusive to lapsed guests. Respect STOP and approved WhatsApp template requirements; do not assume provider messaging is free.

**Measure:** returning paid guests and contribution after messaging and discount costs. Do not measure success by messages sent.

## 9. A real, limited batch people can share

**Business purpose:** create a clear reason to act while keeping the campaign affordable.

**Example setup:** 25 cards with a maximum saving of 500 each, minimum 5,000, activation 0 and validity 7 days. The maximum face-value discount exposure is 12,500 if all 25 are fully redeemed; actual costs and revenue still depend on orders.

**Staff action:** publish the real quantity and terms. If you add another batch, announce it as an additional release; do not use fake countdowns, invented claim activity or permanently “last chance” copy. Extra slots remain unavailable to previous claimants on the same campaign. Create a new campaign for a genuinely new promotion.

**Measure:** time to claim, paid redemption rate and contribution. Fast claims with few paid visits are a weak result.

## 10. Test the message and repeat the profitable winner

**Business purpose:** learn which offer attracts valuable guests instead of guessing.

**Example setup:** run a small, capped campaign with a food-led message one week, then a comparable campaign with a return-visit message. Keep timing, audience and spend threshold similar. Change one main variable at a time. Start with 15–20 cards per experiment.

**Staff action:** use the social publishing source links to compare visits from platforms. Those source counts measure visits, not automatic coupon-to-sale attribution. For campaign results, use the issued card, saved terms, order reference and paid sale. Keep a simple campaign ledger. Two public campaigns can both be claimed by the same network because the restriction is per campaign, so budget accordingly and do not call this a perfectly random A/B test.

**Measure:** paid redemption, first-to-second-visit conversion, average paid basket and contribution. Increase the winning campaign’s budget gradually only when the kitchen can deliver the same quality.

## Protect the margin: one worked example

Suppose a basket sells for 6,000, ingredients and packaging cost 2,600, and other variable fulfilment costs are 400. A 600 coupon leaves:

**6,000 − 600 − 2,600 − 400 = 2,400 contribution before fixed costs.**

Without the coupon, contribution would be 3,000. The coupon therefore costs 600 of contribution on an order that would have happened anyway. Growth is useful when extra paid visits and repeat purchases justify that cost. This is contribution, not net profit; use NVO’s real costs, taxes, payment fees and channel charges in the final calculation. Do not publish these example costs as restaurant facts.

For a free item, forecast both its actual ingredient/packaging cost and any paid sale displaced by giving it away. Set realistic stock and claim limits. Issued coupons retain their original terms even after an editor change, so budget for outstanding valid cards as well as new claims.

## A manageable first month

1. **Week 1:** record usual paid basket size, food cost and repeat visits; publish one strong special and a small welcome campaign.
2. **Week 2:** review paid redemptions and introduce the next-visit offer to satisfied guests.
3. **Week 3:** test either an office basket or a quiet-period offer, whichever fits real capacity.
4. **Week 4:** compare contribution and returning guests. Keep the strongest two campaigns; retire weak ones from new claims while honouring issued cards.

Use a ledger with: campaign name, issue dates, claim cap, issued cards, activated cards, paid redemptions, food subtotal, discount granted, variable cost, net paid amount, first-time/repeat status, source where known and next-visit date. Track this with customer knowledge and keep access limited to the staff who need it.

**Useful calculations:** paid redemption rate = paid redemptions ÷ issued cards; repeat rate = acquired guests who make a second paid purchase ÷ acquired paying guests; campaign contribution = food revenue before discount − discounts − variable costs − campaign/messaging costs. Avoid a percentage where the denominator is zero.

Guest Activity now separates visitor browsers, visits, page views and cart changes. These show interest, not revenue. For sales, use staff-verified paid orders and counter receipts; do not double-count a counter receipt and an order record for the same sale.
